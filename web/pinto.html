What is Pinto

The problem Pinto is trying to solve

Most of the dollars on a blockchain today are stablecoins issued by companies. Each one is backed by money held off the chain, and the company that issues it can freeze any holder's coins. Pinto's team argues that this leaves Ethereum depending on a few central issuers, and calls that "an existential threat to the sovereignty of the Ethereum network."

Pinto starts from three convictions:

  1. Centralised stablecoins are a threat to Ethereum's independence.
  2. Fiat money, money that is printed rather than backed, is the best model, if the printer is used wisely and fairly.
  3. The right incentives can potentially make an on-chain currency stable enough to compete with the centralised ones.

So what is Pinto?

Pinto is a stablecoin on Base that aims for $1. Nothing is locked up behind it. Pinto is built on credit: when there is too much Pinto, the protocol borrows it back from the market and destroys it, and it repays those loans by printing new Pinto later. Pinto calls this "leviathan-free" money: no company holds reserves, and none can freeze holders the way a stablecoin issuer can. Pinto's contract can still be upgraded by a multisig, which is covered further down.

The trade-off, in Pinto's own words: credit "cannot support a perfect peg." Pinto is designed to swing above and below $1 rather than sit on it (Credit vs Collateral). Nothing guarantees it gets back to $1, and Pinto's own Disclosures say so plainly. Pinto launched in November 2024. It is the successor to Beanstalk, which launched on Ethereum in 2021.

The engine

How Pinto holds $1

Pinto keeps time in Seasons of about an hour. Each one starts when someone calls Pinto's sunrise function, for a small Pinto reward (the Sun). At each Season Pinto checks its price across its pools, then acts:

Above $1: too little Pinto

Pinto prints new Pinto. At today's supply, the new Pinto goes 48.5% to repay loans at the front of the Field's line, 48.5% to Silo depositors, and 3% to Pinto's development budget (Minting). With no loans waiting, their share goes to the others.

Below $1: too much Pinto

Pinto offers to borrow Pinto through the Field. Lent Pinto is destroyed, which shrinks the supply, and the lender gets a claim to be repaid later, with interest, if Pinto prints enough.

The Silo

Where Pinto pays its savers

The Silo is Pinto's deposit account. Anyone can deposit Pinto in it and take the Pinto out again whenever they like.

  • Stalk. A deposit earns Stalk, which entitles you to a share of the Silo's part of every future print.
  • Seeds and grown Stalk. Deposits also earn Seeds, which grow more Stalk every Season. The longer you stay, the bigger your share.
  • Germination. A new deposit takes about two Seasons before it earns. This stops anyone from borrowing a large sum, depositing for a moment and taking the yield.
  • Earned Pinto. Your share of each print collects as Earned Pinto. You claim it to add it to your deposit.

The Silo only earns when Pinto prints for savers, and that only happens above $1.

The Field

Where Pinto borrows

The Field is how Pinto borrows when it is below $1.

  • Soil is how much Pinto the protocol will borrow this Season.
  • Sowing is lending: you give Pinto, and it is destroyed.
  • Temperature is the interest rate. Sow 100 Pinto at 50% and you are owed 150.
  • Pods are what you are owed, and each one is worth 1 Pinto when it is repaid.
  • The Pod Line repays loans in order, first in, first out, out of new prints above $1. Harvesting collects Pods that have been repaid.

There is no date on a Pod, and it may never be repaid: it is repaid only if Pinto prints enough to reach its place in the line (Disclosure 6).

Limits

What Chirp can't do

  • Chirp's backing lives inside Pinto. The Pinto behind Chirp sits in Pinto's Silo. Pinto's contract can be upgraded by a 5-of-9 multisig, and Pinto's docs say it is expected to give up that power when it is prudent (Upgradability). A change to Pinto can change what Chirp's Pinto does.
  • Chirp can't be patched. If Pinto ever breaks deposits, withdrawals, sowing or harvesting, Chirp cannot be fixed. Holders keep their coins, but the Pinto would stay deposited in Pinto's Silo, where Chirp may not be able to get it out. A fix would mean a new coin.
  • Nobody is paid to run the upkeep. Claim, Sow and Harvest are public buttons. Anyone can press them, and whoever does is not paid.
  • Sowing uses Pinto's referral program. When Chirp sows, it names the Surco team wallet 0x0dB14dc82CBB0ce820F65c9796E0bC9E19eF08bf as referrer. If Pinto's program applies, Pinto gives that wallet a bonus plot of new Pods and gives Chirp a smaller one. Neither comes out of Chirp's Pinto. This is fixed in the contract and cannot be changed.
